2026 — Present
Research

Aerospace Robotics Laboratory

Undergraduate Researcher · Georgia Institute of Technology

I joined the Aerospace Robotics Laboratory to work on mechanical systems for robotic aerospace platforms. My work has ranged from independently redesigning and integrating a lunar rover chassis and drivetrain to leading development of AeroLatch, a compact mechanism for autonomous aerial docking and future mid-air battery exchange.

The lab has pushed me to treat CAD, manufacturing, electrical integration, accessibility, testing, and controls interfaces as parts of one system rather than isolated design tasks.

Key Contributions

  • Redesigned and integrated a 1,000+ component lunar rover assembly while reducing mass by 20%.
  • Increased rover maximum climbing angle from 23° to 42° through drivetrain and chassis changes.
  • Leading AeroLatch mechanism development from concept selection through physical prototype testing.
Tools & Skills: SolidWorks Design for Manufacturability Mechanical Assembly Electrical Integration Rapid Prototyping

Engineering Leadership

Georgia Tech Design/Build/Fly

Propulsion Lead · Georgia Institute of Technology

As Propulsion Lead, I owned the aircraft propulsion workflow from modeling and component selection through wind-tunnel validation and developmental flight testing. The system had to perform within a 450 W power limit while continuously adapting to changing mass allocations and requirements from other subteams.

I also led a propulsion team and maintained the cross-subteam communication needed to keep propulsion decisions aligned with the complete aircraft.

Key Contributions

  • Improved thrust-to-weight ratio by 18% through propulsion modeling, selection, and testing.
  • Validated configurations with static and dynamic wind-tunnel testing and 100+ developmental flights.
  • Contributed to an aircraft that placed 1st in flight performance at competition.

Research

NASA SEES

Student Researcher · Austin, Texas

Through NASA's STEM Enhancement in Earth Science program, I developed and tested a lunar dust mitigation experiment designed to compare spacesuit fabrics across Earth, Mars, Moon, and microgravity conditions.

The project combined experimental design, mechanical development, rapid redesign under a late mass constraint, reduced-gravity flight testing, data interpretation, and scientific communication.

Key Contributions

  • Redesigned the experiment after the allowable mass dropped from 50 lb to 5 lb shortly before flight.
  • Successfully completed all 16 planned fabric and gravity-environment combinations.
  • Presented the research through scientific communication and first-author at the American Geophysical Union.
Tools & Skills: SolidWorks Experimental Design Rapid Redesign Microgravity Testing Scientific Communication
